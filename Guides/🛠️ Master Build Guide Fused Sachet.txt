🛠️ Master Build Guide: Fused Sachet Desk Organizer
🧰 Tools & Materials Needed
Discarded Water Sachets: 12 to 16 clean, empty sachet water bags.

Parchment / Baking Paper: 2 large sheets (crucial—this prevents melted plastic from sticking to your iron!).

Household Clothes Iron: Set to medium/wool heat.

Scissors or Utility Knife: For trimming straight edges.

Ruler & Marker: For measuring layout lines.

Adhesive or Thread: Strong contact adhesive (like shoe glue/epoxy) or a heavy-duty needle and thread.

🎛️ Step-by-Step Construction Process
Step 1: Prep & Sanitize the Plastic
Cut open the top and bottom seals of your empty sachet water bags and slice down one side to lay each bag flat into a rectangle.

Wash the plastic sheets thoroughly with soap and water to remove any residual moisture or dirt, then dry them completely with a towel. (Any leftover water will trap steam inside the plastic layers and create bubbles!)

Step 2: Layer the Stack
Lay one sheet of parchment paper flat on a smooth, heat-resistant table or ironing board.

Stack 6 to 8 layers of flattened sachet plastic directly on top of each other over the parchment paper.

Pro Tip: Alternate the direction of the sachets so the printed logos overlap randomly—this creates a cool, abstract graphic pattern on your final sheet!

Cover the top of the plastic stack with your second sheet of parchment paper so the plastic is completely sandwiched inside.

Step 3: Thermal Fusion (Ironing)
Set your iron to medium heat (the wool/silk setting works best).

Press the iron down firmly onto the top parchment paper, moving it constantly in slow, circular motions for 10 to 15 seconds.

Flip the entire parchment paper sandwich over and iron the reverse side for another 10 to 15 seconds.

Let it cool for 30 seconds, then peel away the parchment paper. You will be left with a single, thick, durable, and flexible sheet of waterproof plastic leather!

Step 4: Measure & Cut the Tray Pattern
Take your fused plastic sheet and trim the rough outer edges using a ruler and utility knife to create a clean 20 cm x 15 cm rectangle.

Measure 3 cm inward from all four edges and draw light fold lines. These 3 cm borders will fold upward to form the 4 walls of your desk tray.

At each of the four corners, make a single 3 cm cut along one fold line so the corner tabs can overlap when folded.

Step 5: Fold, Bond & Compartment Build
Fold all four outer edges upward along your marked lines to create a shallow box shape.

Apply a drop of strong adhesive (or stitch with a needle and thread) to secure the overlapping corner tabs firmly in place.

Optional Divider Wall: Take leftover fused plastic scraps, cut a 15 cm x 3 cm strip, fold small 0.5 cm tabs on the ends, and glue it across the center of your tray to create two separate compartments (one for sticky notes/flash drives and one for keys/cables).
